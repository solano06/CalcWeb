Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Phasor Algebra & Sinusoidal Steady-State Analysis

In AC Circuit Theory, the Steinmetz phasor transform converts linear integro-differential equations under sinusoidal excitation v(t) = V_m cos(ωt + φ) into complex linear algebraic systems where inductors have impedance Z_L = jωL and capacitors have impedance Z_C = -j/(ωC).

  • To enter a polar phasor with angle in degrees, use '<' or '∠': for example 10<30 or 10∠-45.
  • Before adding or subtracting two polar phasors (e.g. 5<30 + 4<-60), always convert them to rectangular form a + bj.
  • In a passive AC mesh system without dependent sources, the impedance matrix Z is symmetric across the main diagonal.

Frequently Asked Questions about Phasors & AC Circuits

How do you convert a complex number from rectangular (a + bj) to polar (M∠θ°) form?

Compute magnitude via Pythagoras M = √(a² + b²) and angle via arctan(b/a) with quadrant adjustment: if real part 'a' is negative (2nd or 3rd quadrant), add 180° (for b ≥ 0) or subtract 180° (for b < 0).

How do you convert a polar phasor (M∠θ°) to rectangular form?

Apply Euler's formula M·e^{jθ} = M·cos(θ) + j·M·sin(θ), where a = M·cos(θ) and b = M·sin(θ) with θ in degrees.

Which form is best for adding, subtracting, multiplying, or dividing phasors?

Use rectangular form (a + bj) for addition and subtraction, and polar form (M∠θ°) for multiplication and division.

How do you solve AC mesh or nodal equations?

Form the complex matrix equation Z·I = V using impedances R, jωL, and -j/(ωC), then apply complex Cramer's rule or Gaussian elimination to obtain each current in polar form I_m∠φ°.

Numerical Series (Convergence Tests & Exact Sum)

Rigorous step-by-step convergence analysis: necessary condition lim aₙ = 0, geometric series, p-series, D'Alembert's ratio test, Cauchy's root test, Leibniz alternating test, and exact sum.

Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Convergence Tests & Infinite Series Summation

Analyzing an infinite numerical series ∑ aₙ consists of determining whether the sequence of partial sums S_N converges to a finite limit as N → ∞. Always start by checking the necessary condition: if lim_{n→∞} aₙ ≠ 0, the series diverges immediately.

  • Always check lim_{n→∞} a_n first: if it is non-zero, the series diverges in a single step.
  • If the general term is a rational quotient P(n)/Q(n), compare asymptotically with the p-series 1/n^{deg(Q) - deg(P)}.
  • For a geometric series ∑ r^n starting at n = n₀, the exact sum is always (first term) / (1 - ratio) = r^{n₀} / (1 - r).

Frequently Asked Questions about Numerical Series

If the limit of the general term lim a_n equals 0, does the series always converge?

No. Having lim_{n→∞} a_n = 0 is a necessary condition, NOT a sufficient one. For example, the harmonic series ∑ 1/n satisfies lim 1/n = 0 yet diverges to infinity.

When should you use D'Alembert's Ratio Test vs. Cauchy's Root Test?

Use D'Alembert's Ratio Test (lim |a_{n+1}/a_n|) when a_n involves factorials (n!) or products, and Cauchy's Root Test (lim ⁿ√|a_n|) when the entire term is raised to the power n.

What is the difference between absolute convergence and conditional convergence?

A series converges absolutely if ∑ |a_n| converges. It converges conditionally if ∑ a_n converges (e.g., by Leibniz's test) while ∑ |a_n| diverges (such as ∑ (-1)^{n+1}/n = ln 2).

How do you find the exact sum of a geometric or telescoping series?

For a convergent geometric series (|r| < 1) starting at n0, S = r^{n0} / (1 - r). For a telescoping series like ∑ 1/(n(n+1)), expand into partial fractions 1/n - 1/(n+1) so intermediate terms cancel.

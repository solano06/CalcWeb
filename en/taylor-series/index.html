Taylor & Maclaurin Series Step-by-Step

Systematic computation of successive derivatives f⁽ᵏ⁾(x), evaluation at the expansion center x₀, and term-by-term assembly of the Taylor polynomial Pₙ(x).

Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Taylor Polynomials, Maclaurin Series & Lagrange Remainder

Taylor's Theorem approximates any n-times differentiable function around a center x₀ by a polynomial Pₙ(x) whose derivatives of orders 0 through n match those of f(x) at x = x₀. When centered at the origin (x₀ = 0), it is called a Maclaurin Series.

  • Enter the function, expansion center x₀, and order n separated by semicolons: f(x) ; x0 ; n (for example: ln(x) ; 1 ; 4).
  • If a function is even (like cos(x)), all odd derivatives at x₀ = 0 vanish; if it is odd (like sin(x)), all even derivatives vanish.
  • To evaluate indeterminate 0/0 limits, replace each function by its Taylor polynomial up to the first non-zero term.

Frequently Asked Questions about Taylor & Maclaurin Series

What is the difference between a Taylor Series and a Maclaurin Series?

A Maclaurin Series is simply the special case of a Taylor Series centered at the origin x0 = 0, expanding in powers of x^k rather than (x - x0)^k.

How are the coefficients of a Taylor Polynomial of order n computed?

For each k from 0 to n, compute the k-th derivative f^(k)(x), evaluate it at x0 to get f^(k)(x0), and divide by k factorial: c_k = f^(k)(x0) / k!.

Why can't ln(x) be expanded as a Maclaurin series at x0 = 0?

Because ln(x) and its derivatives (-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!/x^k are undefined at x = 0. Instead, ln(x) is expanded around x0 = 1, or ln(1 + x) is expanded around x0 = 0.

How do you bound the approximation error of a Taylor Polynomial?

Using the Lagrange Remainder R_n(x) = f^(n+1)(ξ) / (n+1)! · (x - x0)^(n+1) for some ξ between x0 and x.

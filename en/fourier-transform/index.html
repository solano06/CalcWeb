Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Fourier Transform in Engineering

In Signals & Systems and Telecommunications, the continuous-time Fourier Transform decomposes an aperiodic signal f(t) into its angular frequency spectral density F(ω). Generalized distributions such as the Dirac Delta δ(ω) extend the transform to periodic and DC signals.

Direct & Inverse Definition (Angular Frequency ω Convention)

F(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega

The normalization factor 1/(2π) appears exclusively in the inverse synthesis integral.

Unilateral Causal Exponential (RC Low-Pass Filter)

\mathcal{F}\left\{e^{-at}u(t)\right\} = \frac{1}{a + j\omega}, \qquad \text{para } \operatorname{Re}(a) > 0

The Heaviside step u(t) truncates the integral to [0, ∞). Its magnitude is 1/√(a² + ω²) and its phase is -arctan(ω/a).

Rectangular Pulse & Sinc Duality

\mathcal{F}\left\{A\,\text{rect}\!\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\right\} = A T\,\text{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\omega T}{2}\right) = \frac{2A\sin(\omega T / 2)}{\omega}

The narrower the pulse in time (smaller T), the wider its main lobe in frequency (Uncertainty Principle).

Harmonic Oscillators & Dirac Deltas

\mathcal{F}\left\{\cos(\omega_0 t)\right\} = \pi\left[\delta(\omega - \omega_0) + \delta(\omega + \omega_0)\right], \qquad \mathcal{F}\left\{\delta(t - t_0)\right\} = e^{-j\omega t_0}

Derived by applying Euler's formula cos(ω₀t) = (e^{jω₀t} + e^{-jω₀t})/2 and the frequency-shift property.

  • Always check whether the signal is multiplied by the unit step u(t): this changes the lower integration limit from -∞ to 0.
  • When transforming pure undamped trigonometric functions, use Euler's identity to rewrite them as complex exponentials.
  • A time delay t → (t - t₀) does not alter the magnitude spectrum |F(ω)|; it only adds a linear phase shift -ω·t₀.

Frequently Asked Questions about Fourier Transform

How do you compute the Fourier Transform of a causal exponential e^(-at)u(t)?

Because the Heaviside step u(t) vanishes for t < 0, the integral limits reduce from (-∞, ∞) to [0, ∞). Combining the exponents yields the integral of e^{-(a + jω)t}, which converges for Re(a) > 0 to F(ω) = 1 / (a + jω).

What is the Fourier Transform of a rectangular pulse rect(t/T)?

A rectangular pulse of amplitude A and width T centered at the origin equals A on [-T/2, T/2]. Integrating e^{-jωt} over that interval and applying Euler's identity yields the sinc function: F(ω) = A·T·sinc(ωT/2) = (2A·sin(ωT/2))/ω.

Why does the Fourier Transform of cos(ω0·t) contain Dirac Deltas?

Because cos(ω0·t) has infinite energy and is not absolutely integrable. Using Euler's formula cos(ω0·t) = (e^{jω0t} + e^{-jω0t})/2 concentrates its spectral energy into two Dirac impulses at ±ω0: F(ω) = π[δ(ω - ω0) + δ(ω + ω0)].

What is the difference between Fourier Transform in angular frequency ω and frequency f (Hz)?

In the angular frequency ω = 2πf convention, the factor 1/(2π) appears only in the inverse synthesis integral, and the transform of a constant 1 is 2π·δ(ω).

Fourier Series (Trigonometric a₀, aₙ, bₙ & Complex Exponential cₙ)

Harmonic decomposition of periodic signals on [-L, L] with automatic even/odd symmetry detection, explicit coefficient integrals, and N-harmonic partial sum.

Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Fourier Series & Symmetry Conditions

Any periodic signal of period T = 2L satisfying Dirichlet's conditions can be expanded as an infinite superposition of harmonic sinusoids at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency ω₀ = π/L. Checking for even or odd parity before integrating saves half the work in exams.

  • Before integrating by parts, always check if f(-x) = f(x) (Even, so b_n = 0) or f(-x) = -f(x) (Odd, so a_0 = a_n = 0).
  • When evaluating at the endpoints of [-π, π], use the identities cos(nπ) = (-1)^n and sin(nπ) = 0 for every integer n.
  • At jump discontinuities of f(x), the Fourier series converges to the midpoint of the left and right limits: (f(x⁺) + f(x⁻))/2.

Frequently Asked Questions about Fourier Series

How can you tell if Fourier coefficients an or bn are zero without integrating?

By checking parity on a symmetric interval [-L, L]. If f(x) is EVEN (f(-x) = f(x), like x² or |x|), then all sine coefficients bn = 0. If f(x) is ODD (f(-x) = -f(x), like x or sgn(x)), then a0 = 0 and all cosine coefficients an = 0.

How do you convert trigonometric coefficients (a0, an, bn) to complex exponential coefficients (cn)?

Using Euler's formulas: c0 = a0 / 2, cn = (an - j·bn) / 2 for n ≥ 1, and c_{-n} = (an + j·bn) / 2 (the complex conjugate of cn for real signals).

Why is cos(n·π) replaced by (-1)^n in Fourier Series?

Because for integer n, cos(π) = -1, cos(2π) = 1, cos(3π) = -1, which alternates signs exactly as (-1)^n, whereas sin(nπ) = 0 for all integers.

How do you use a Fourier Series to evaluate numerical series like ∑ 1/n²?

Evaluate the Fourier series at a continuity point (such as x = π or x = 0 for f(x) = x²) or apply Parseval's Theorem.

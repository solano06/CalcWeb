Ordinary Differential Equations (1st & 2nd Order ODEs)

Analytical step-by-step resolution of separable ODEs, 1st-order linear ODEs via integrating factor μ(x), and 2nd-order constant-coefficient ODEs with Initial Value Problems (IVP).

Quick Guide & Key Formulas: Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)

Ordinary Differential Equations model physical dynamical systems, RLC circuits, and thermal processes. University analytical resolution classifies ODEs according to their order and linearity.

  • Never apply initial conditions y(0), y'(0) to the homogeneous solution y_h(x) alone: always form the complete general solution y(x) = y_h(x) + y_p(x) first.
  • In undetermined coefficients, if the forcing term e^{kx} matches a characteristic root r = k of multiplicity m, multiply the trial solution by x^m.
  • For a 1st-order linear ODE a(x)y' + b(x)y = c(x), divide by a(x) first so y' has leading coefficient 1 before identifying P(x).

Frequently Asked Questions about Differential Equations (ODEs)

How do you solve a first-order linear differential equation using an integrating factor?

Write the ODE in standard form y' + P(x)y = Q(x) and compute μ(x) = e^{∫ P(x) dx}. Multiplying the equation by μ(x) collapses the left side into d/dx[μ(x)·y], so integrating both sides gives y(x) = (∫ μ(x)Q(x) dx + C) / μ(x).

How do you build the homogeneous solution of a 2nd-order linear ODE?

Solve the characteristic equation a·r² + b·r + c = 0. Distinct real roots r1, r2 give C1·e^{r1x} + C2·e^{r2x}; a repeated root r gives (C1 + C2·x)e^{rx}; complex conjugate roots α ± jβ give e^{αx}(C1·cos(βx) + C2·sin(βx)).

When should initial conditions y(0) and y'(0) be substituted in a non-homogeneous ODE?

Always substitute initial conditions into the total general solution y(x) = yh(x) + yp(x), never into yh(x) alone.

How do you recognize a separable first-order ODE?

An ODE is separable if it can be written as dy/dx = f(x)·g(y). Separate variables as dy/g(y) = f(x)dx and integrate both sides independently.
